## The Book That Made Me Rethink About Purpose

I recently finished reading _So Good They Can’t Ignore You_ by Cal Newport, a book recommended by [Julián Campos](https://open.substack.com/users/173193757-julian-campos?utm_source=mentions) on a brilliant X/Twitter thread about books every developer should read.

Book cover.

This was the book that really hit me this year.

Why? Cause all my life I’ve thought the popular advice “find your passion and the rest will follow” was real. Too many decisions made using this mantra.

Then, to my surprise, Newport argues the opposite in the chapters of this book.

Telling stories of people from each path and ultimately showing who developed more successful and satisfying lives.

Here’s the deal.

## Passion Mindset vs. Craft Mindset

The central subject of the book is these two concepts.

The passion mindset is about finding work that matches a passion you supposedly already have. It’s asking “what can the world offer me?” and assuming passion comes first, then the work follows.

The craft mindset flips this. It’s about getting really good at something rare and valuable. Asking “what can I offer?” instead.

The key difference: **passion comes after you develop mastery, not before.**

This made me rethink how I approach my own career in tech. Since I pivoted from medicine I’ve been unknowingly following this path, trying to get better at managing data, programming and ultimately building AI systems.

I was lucky enough to apply it to football (which I love) and healthcare, my background.

But the true passion came from pushing through the work and wanting to get better each day.

#### How Chalamet Pursues Greatness Through Craft

In February, at the SAG Awards ceremony, Timothée Chalamet said something unusual: “I’m really in pursuit of greatness. I want to be one of the greats.”

I felt that quote was really honest because he was actually talking about becoming great through the work.

He mentioned being inspired by Daniel Day-Lewis, Marlon Brando, and Viola Davis, and spending 5.5 years of his life preparing for _A Complete Unknown_ where he portrayed Bob Dylan.

That’s the craft mindset in action.

Greatness is something you build deliberately over years of committed work.

## Career Capital: The Currency to Buy What You Want in Your Career

Career capital is the set of rare and valuable skills you’ve accumulated through the years.

Newport uses this economic metaphor deliberately: great jobs (those offering creativity, impact, and control) are scarce and valuable.

To get one, you need to offer something scarce and valuable in return.

Most people want autonomy, interesting projects, and meaningful work from day one (happened to me). But you need currency to buy those things. First you accumulate the capital, then you invest it to negotiate the career you actually want.

You build it by developing skills that are rare and valuable, ones others don’t have and the market needs.

This changed how I choose what to learn.

Before, I chased new technologies that sounded interesting but now I ask myself if it gives me the career capital I want to build. Or is it rare and valuable, more than a commodity.

For example: Python is valuable but not rare. Instead, using Python to create AI systems applied to a niche domain like football analytics or healthcare is a rare combination.

This lens shows if you're building capital or just chasing noise.
