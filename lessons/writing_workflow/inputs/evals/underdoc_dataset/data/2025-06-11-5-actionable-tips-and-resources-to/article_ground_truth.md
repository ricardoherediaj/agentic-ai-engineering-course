# 5 Actionable Tips and Resources to Start Analyzing Football Data

*Personal lessons I've learned since diving into football data projects*

Like many, I'm passionate about football. A few years ago, I discovered how to extract real value from match data.

Today I bring 5 tips that helped me get acquainted with how football data is used in real-world settings.

#### 1\. Start Where the Data Is

You don't need special credentials to start. High-quality datasets are readily available on public websites.

Some of them:

- StatsBomb: Granular event data for advanced analysis of top competitions and players (Messi’s career, Euro, Copa America, Leverkusen unbeaten Bundesliga 2023/2024). [StatsBomb Free Data](https://statsbomb.com/what-we-do/hub/free-data/)

- FBRef: Aggregated stats for team and player comparisons, trend tracking, and scouting. [FBRef](https://fbref.com/en/)

- Transfermarkt: The go-to for player profiles, transfer histories, market values, and club finances. [Transfermark](https://www.transfermarkt.en/)

- WhoScored: Detailed match and player stats, including event-by-event breakdowns and player ratings. [WhoScored (Opta Data)](https://www.whoscored.com/)

- For Video Analysis: Ben Griffis Streaming sheet is the go to: [Ben Griffis Free Streaming Sheet](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/18QxXLSGo_gqU0lKApfkQOkW9Z-O0sj0AfFNECAbCUOk/edit?gid=0#gid=0)

Spend time exploring these resources, ask yourself real football questions, and let the data guide your analysis.

Data sources I use and have used before.

#### 2\. Free YouTube Channels

If I had to pick three channels to binge-watch, it would be:

- Friends of Tracking: Community-driven project with free educational content, code, and data. Led by David Sumpter one of the data pioneers in the field. [Friends of Tracking](https://www.youtube.com/@friendsoftracking755/featured)

- McKay Johns: sTutorials, code, and guides on using Python and data science for football analysis. [McKay Johns Channel](https://www.youtube.com/@McKayJohns) He has football analytics courses that are well crafted and contains everything you need to know to start creating quality projects.

- LanusStats: this one is in Spanish, the creator Federico Rábanos teaches how to use Python to grab stats, make dashboards, and understand metrics. It's great if you speak Spanish and want to learn how to analyze soccer with data and code. [LanusStats](https://www.youtube.com/@LanusStats)

YouTube Channels you can watch.

#### 3\. Improve Your Football Knowledge

It's easy to overestimate our football knowledge. Coaches, analysts, and scouts use specific tactical concepts daily.

For that, I recommend:

- Watch games with a tactical focus: observe team behavior in attack, defense, build-up play, and player technique. Go beyond just following the ball. You can watch free matches in these leagues: [Ben Griffis Google Sheet](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/18QxXLSGo_gqU0lKApfkQOkW9Z-O0sj0AfFNECAbCUOk/edit), a list of leagues that upload their matches put up together by another great analyst Ben Griffis who also shares awesome visualizations and resources ( [Ben Griffis X Account)](https://x.com/BeGriffis)

- Online resources: watch YouTube channels that talk about tactics, read blogs in Medium and Substack or even books that go deep in these concepts.

- At some point and if its suitable for your pocket start with affordable video analysis courses that can help you level up your game understanding. For this I’ve taken two courses (in Spanish):

  - **AFOPRO (Spanish)**: an online academy that teaches a wide variety of courses in scouting, video analysis, physical preparation at affordable prices. [AFOPRO](https://afopro.com/)

  - **ObjetivoAnalista (Spanish)**: one of the best academies on Spain created by Dani Pérez, they offer three signature course: Analista360, Scouting360 and PowerBI 360. I’ve personally taken Analista360 and Scouting360. The first one help me A LOT to improve my game understanding and applying this knowledge to a collaborative opportunity I had as a remote videoanalysis for a friend’s team in the US. I’m currently finishing Scouting360 because I want to get deeper in scouting analytics. [ObjetivoAnalista](https://objetivoanalista.com/)

  - **APFA (English):** anorganization focused on developping analysis skills, foster a community through webinars and content, provide career progression resources like job opportunities and mentorship. [APFA](https://apfa.io/)

Analysis courses I’ve personally taken.

Technical skills matter but there’s something that’s really important to consider: knowing the game matters too.

Picture it like this: you think in data, but speak in football.

#### 4\. Start Building Projects: From Simple to End-to-End Solutions

Building projects is the best way to learn. You don't need to know everything upfront; start with what you know..

It doesn’t matter if your first project is simple. A simple visualization from one match or a scouting report from one player. You improve by building tiny projects until you gain confidence through creating. In time, you’ll build more complex data pipelines or modeling.

The important is to:

- Show your entire process: data collection, cleaning, modeling, and visualization.

- Communicate your insights in a clean and concise way.

- Translate everything into football terms.

#### 5\. Share Your Work, Build Your Network

Sharing my work was a challenge at first. I had accounts on X and LinkedIn, but rarely posted. It's easy to feel hesitant.

I started being a little bit more constant. Since there’s value in sharing what we know. It’ll probably going to help somebody else realize they like football analytics and creating content online like I did back when I did my first Python course with DataCamp (a story for another day).

The are plenty of platforms you can use but I recommend using:

- X: Share quick insights, visualizations, and engage with the community.

- LinkedIn: Showcase professional projects, network with potential employers, and share career progress.

- Medium/Substack (obviously): Share in-depth articles, build a community, and connect with readers. Substack is a recent one for me but I’m digging it.
